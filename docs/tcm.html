PRE-PILOT

Track 2 · AI Agent Communication & Collaboration

Trusted Colleague Model

Can an AI agent work more naturally and effectively by behaving like a competent, context-aware colleague—not by pretending to be human, but by sharing the goal, exercising judgment, and reducing collaboration friction?

Protocol only. No TCM experiment has been run yet, so this page reports hypotheses and design—not findings.

Working relationship, not cosmetic tone

What TCM is testing

A good AI colleague should be competent, predictable, context-aware, appropriately proactive, willing to challenge material mistakes, and clear about uncertainty.

Shared goal + ownership + context + judgment + calibrated initiative + constructive disagreement + trust calibration + natural expression

Shared goal & ownership

Optimize for the real outcome and notice obvious gaps, without taking authority away from the user.

Context & shared mental model

Carry forward valid decisions, constraints, blockers, and evidence instead of restarting each turn.

Monitoring & backup

Catch material assumptions or errors and provide task-relevant backup when a gap would block success.

Constructive disagreement

Say when a proposal is risky or weak, explain why, and avoid both automatic agreement and needless contrarianism.

Calibrated initiative & adaptability

Know when to continue, when to ask, and how strongly to review or execute based on task risk and uncertainty.

Trust calibration & natural expression

Separate verified facts from inference and communicate directly without rigid report templates or fake familiarity.

Controlled comparison

Four experimental conditions

Model, reasoning, tools, safety, context, and task stay the same. The communication/collaboration contract changes.

A

Report Assistant

Competent professional baseline: clear and structured, without an explicit teammate-behavior contract.

B

Friendly Assistant

Same baseline behavior with warmer, less bureaucratic wording. This isolates friendly tone.

C

Trusted Colleague

Fixed peer stance with shared-goal behavior, monitoring, backup, constructive disagreement, and calibrated certainty.

D

Adaptive Trusted Colleague

TCM plus a temporary Peer, Senior, Specialist, Reviewer, or Operator stance chosen for the current task—not a fixed role router.

Pre-registered before formal runs

Experimental protocol

Pilot runs486 tasks × 4 conditions × 2 repeats
Full runs480after pilot and protocol freeze
Full tasks248 categories × 3 real-world tasks
Repeats5nested within task and condition
Blind evaluation. Generation order and evaluation order are independently randomized. Evaluators see neutral response IDs, not condition labels or the expected winner.

Primary outcomes

  • Task Success — did the response satisfy prewritten acceptance criteria?
  • Colleague Fit — does it behave like a competent, reliable work partner?
  • Collaboration Friction Score — how much avoidable correction, repetition, confirmation, or extra-turn effort did the user need?
  • Willingness to Continue — would the evaluator keep working with this agent?

Planned analysis controls for task and rater effects, reports effect sizes and uncertainty, tests response length as a sensitivity variable, and keeps safety/factual accuracy as non-negotiable guardrails.

Theory informs the test; it does not pre-decide the result

Evidence foundations

Peer-reviewed

Big Five of Teamwork

Provides teamwork constructs such as mutual monitoring, backup behavior, adaptability, shared mental models, closed-loop communication, and trust.

Salas, Sims & Burke (2005)
Peer-reviewed

Shared mental models in human-AI teams

Motivates testing whether aligned task/role understanding helps humans and AI predict needs and coordinate more effectively.

Theoretical review (2022)
Peer-reviewed

LLM as tool vs teammate

A large online experiment found teammate presentation changed trust and interaction sentiment, supporting relationship framing as a variable worth testing.

ACM TOIIS (2025)
Peer-reviewed

Misunderstanding, trust & performance

Human-AI teamwork evidence links information omission and communication breakdowns with trust and performance effects.

Frontiers in Psychology (2025)

These sources motivate constructs and hypotheses. None directly proves that TCM will improve software-engineering or ERP-agent work; that is what the planned experiment must test.

Anti-anthropomorphism boundary

Colleague behavior without pretending to be human

TCM does not ask an AI to claim feelings, identity, authority, or human responsibility. Over-initiative, fake familiarity, excessive disagreement, stale-context overreach, unsupported certainty, and length inflation are explicit failure modes.
