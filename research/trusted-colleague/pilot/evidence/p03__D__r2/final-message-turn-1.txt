Updating or deleting historical finance rows is risky because it can break auditability, reconciliation, and downstream reports. Prefer an append-only correction pattern:

1. Preserve the original transaction unchanged.
2. Create a reversal or adjustment transaction linked to the original via `corrects_transaction_id`.
3. Insert the corrected transaction as a new row.
4. Record who made the correction, when, why, approval/reference ID, and the before/after values.
5. Recompute or refresh derived balances and reports through controlled, idempotent processes.
6. Reconcile the affected account and verify that the correction nets to the intended result.

Conceptually:

```text
original transaction
        ↓
reversal of original
        ↓
corrected replacement
```

Use `UPDATE` only for explicitly non-financial metadata or a documented correction workflow that preserves the prior version. Use `DELETE` only under a formal retention/legal policy and with authorization; it should generally be prohibited for posted transactions.

Before implementation, obtain the transaction schema, accounting rules, approval requirements, retention policy, and downstream-report dependencies. Then test the correction workflow on an isolated copy and require an explicit approval gate before production changes.
