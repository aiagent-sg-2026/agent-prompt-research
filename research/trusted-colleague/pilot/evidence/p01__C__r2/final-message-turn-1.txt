The likely cause is row multiplication: one invoice joins to multiple `invoice_lines` and multiple `customer_tags`, producing a many-to-many combination of child rows. For example, 3 lines × 2 tags can yield 6 rows for one invoice.

`SELECT DISTINCT` is risky because it only hides identical projected rows and can silently discard meaningful differences or mask incorrect totals.

Safest next step: define the intended grain as one row per invoice, then aggregate or pre-aggregate each child relationship separately before joining:

- Aggregate invoice lines by `invoice_id` for totals, quantities, or counts.
- Aggregate customer tags by customer/invoice key, or use an existence/filter condition if only tag presence matters.
- Join those one-row-per-invoice results to `invoices`.
- Validate invoice counts and financial totals against a trusted source.
