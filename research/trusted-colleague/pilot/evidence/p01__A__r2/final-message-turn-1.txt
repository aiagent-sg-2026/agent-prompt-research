The likely cause is join multiplication: each invoice with multiple invoice lines and multiple customer tags produces every line–tag combination, so one invoice becomes many rows.

`SELECT DISTINCT` may hide symptoms but can discard legitimate differences and mask incorrect totals.

Safest next step: preserve the intended grain—one row per invoice—by aggregating `invoice_lines` and `customer_tags` separately before joining them to `invoices`. If either table is only used for filtering, use `EXISTS` instead of joining. Then validate invoice counts and monetary totals against the source data.
