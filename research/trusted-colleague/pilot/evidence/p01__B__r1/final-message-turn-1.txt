The likely cause is join multiplication: each invoice line is combined with each customer tag, producing multiple rows per invoice—potentially `line_count × tag_count`.

`SELECT DISTINCT` may hide duplicates but can also mask incorrect totals and discard legitimate differences. The safest next step is to confirm the report’s intended grain—one row per invoice—then aggregate or pre-aggregate `invoice_lines` and `customer_tags` separately before joining them to `invoices`.

For example:

```sql
FROM invoices i
LEFT JOIN (
  SELECT invoice_id, ...
  FROM invoice_lines
  GROUP BY invoice_id
) l ON l.invoice_id = i.id
LEFT JOIN (
  SELECT customer_id, ...
  FROM customer_tags
  GROUP BY customer_id
) t ON t.customer_id = i.customer_id
```

Validate the revised query by checking that it returns at most one row per invoice and that financial totals remain correct.
