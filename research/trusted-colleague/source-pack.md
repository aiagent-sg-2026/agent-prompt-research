# TCM verified source pack

Last checked: 2026-09-02. This source pack separates foundational teamwork evidence from human-AI / LLM evidence. It does not treat adjacent findings as direct causal proof that TCM will work.

## Foundational teamwork research

### Salas, Sims & Burke (2005) — Is there a “Big Five” in Teamwork?

URL: https://doi.org/10.1177/1046496405277134

Peer-reviewed in *Small Group Research*. The framework identifies team leadership, mutual performance monitoring, backup behavior, adaptability, and team orientation as five core teamwork components, supported by shared mental models, closed-loop communication, and mutual trust.

**Use in TCM:** supplies established teamwork constructs for operationalizing colleague-like collaboration.

**Boundary:** human-team theory is not evidence that an LLM implementing these behaviors will necessarily improve outcomes.

### Shared mental models in human-AI teams (2022)

URL: https://doi.org/10.1080/1463922X.2022.2061080

A theoretical review links shared mental model quality in human teams to performance and develops a conceptual application to human-AI teams, while emphasizing unresolved measurement and formation questions.

**Use in TCM:** motivates Context Continuity / Shared Mental Model as a testable dimension.

**Boundary:** theoretical review, not a direct test of TCM or conversational LLM agents.

## Human-AI / LLM evidence

### Flathmann et al. (2025) — LLMs as Tools and Teammates

URL: https://doi.org/10.1145/3764591

Peer-reviewed ACM TOIIS online experiment (N=778). Presenting a commercially available LLM as a teammate rather than a tool increased trust and affected interaction sentiment; participant prior experience also predicted perceptions.

**Use in TCM:** supports testing relationship framing and teammate behavior as meaningful experimental variables rather than treating interface wording as irrelevant.

**Boundary:** framing effects do not establish that TCM's specific behaviors improve objective task performance.

### Chen & Zhang (2025) — Misunderstanding, trust, communication and performance

URL: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1637339

Peer-reviewed experiment with 126 participants using a 2×2 human-AI/human-human and misunderstanding-type design. AI information omissions reduced team trust, which was associated with worse communication efficiency and performance; ambiguous-expression effects depended on trust.

**Use in TCM:** supports explicit measurement of information omission, ambiguity, trust calibration, and communication efficiency/friction.

**Boundary:** one collaborative writing/planning setting; it does not directly validate software-engineering agent behavior.

## Evidence synthesis

The defensible starting point is not “make AI sound human.” Teamwork research suggests collaboration depends on monitoring, backup, adaptation, shared understanding, communication, and trust; human-AI evidence suggests teammate framing and communication failures can alter trust and interaction. TCM therefore treats these as hypotheses and measurable mechanisms, then requires controlled experiments before product claims.
